## The hazard patcher was blind to branch consumers

`tools/hazard_scan.py` and `tools/hazard_patch.py` took the first operand
of every non-store instruction as its destination, so a consumer such as
`beqz a2, T` or `bne t5, v0, T` was never recognised as reading the
register a delay-slot load had just written. The new memcmp, whose first
instruction tested `a2`, returned zero for every call whose caller's `jal`
slot loaded the count, and Cortex ran +52% cycles with different hashes.

Rescanning the 2026-09-03 Cortex exe, which the old tool had reported
clean, found:

- `CharacterBlockerTraceProvider::trace_into +0xe4`: `beqz s1` with `lw v1`
  in the slot, fall-through `beqz v1`.
- `ui::resolved_from_parent +0x4d0`: `bne t8, v0` with `lw t5` in the slot,
  fall-through `bne t5, v0`.
- `render3d::submit_textured_triangle_leaf +0x254`: a `jr at` switch whose
  slot loads `a1` and whose table targets 0, 1, 2 and 7 read `a1` first.

The tools now treat every conditional branch as reading all its registers,
resolve a `jr` jump table (the last `lui` before the `lw rs, off(...)` plus
the offset) and repoint hazardous table entries, which are data words, at
`nop; j T; nop` trampolines, and warn on a `jalr` whose slot loads an
argument register (callee unknown). The jump-table fix changes the frame:
the shadow decal under the player's feet, missing before, draws again
(`docs/overnight-perf-2026-09-02/hazardfix-tapeend-ab.png`). The asm mem
routines start with one `nop` so a `jalr` caller can never expose them.
hl-psx's vendored `host/hazard_patch.py` carries the same fix. quake,
VoXide, NitroXide, PSXcel, gh-psx and pico8 build with
`-disable-mips-df-backward-search` and were never exposed.

Verification probe: `make hello-memprobe`, then
`frontend launch --path build/examples/mipsel-sony-psx/release/hello-memprobe.exe --steps 80000000`
prints `MEMPROBE PASS cases=000014D4` (5,332 size/alignment/guard cases).

## Why quake's wins do not port to Cortex (and what does)

Manny asked why the two games share so much and yet the night's quake gains
do not reach Cortex. Measured per presented gameplay frame:

| | Cortex 0.4 tape | quake E1M1 chain |
|---|---:|---:|
| hardware primitives (tris + quads) | 834 (668 + 166) | 1,197 (910 + 287) |
| world-pass instructions | 209k | 262k |
| instructions per world primitive | ~337 | ~228 |
| model instructions | 159k (~190 skinned tris drawn, ~245 culled) | 37k (alias, ~50 tris) |
| total work instructions | 571k | 490k |

What is shared: the SDK (GTE, GPU packets, OT link, pad, CD, the new mem
routines), psx-engine's classic-affine writer and attributed clip, and
psx-bsp's collision, movers and formats. What is forked: the world
traversal, visibility, materialise and sky. psx-bsp's `render.rs` was lifted
from quake's renderer at commit 83a6349 and both have moved since (quake:
water portals, liquids, sprites, 62 renderer flags; Cortex: PXBSP node
boxes, clip-mask inheritance, plane-side table, retire-by-clear). hl-psx is
a third, unrelated pipeline.

Quake's shipping renderer flags against Cortex, one by one:

- selection cache (exact camera key): quake's first-person camera is still
  between digital inputs, so the previous frame's list is reused 77% of the
  time; Cortex's third-person camera eases every frame, so the key never
  repeats. Cortex's analogue is the alternate-frame selection reuse it has.
- block frustum / screen frustum: Cortex culls with hierarchical PXBSP node
  boxes and inherited clip masks (the 32-unit grid fix made that live), and
  its side planes come from the exact half-height. Nothing to add.
- GPU polygon clip: measured on Cortex, -0.22%, dropped as noise.
- GTE near classification: Cortex has the GTE per-vertex scan.
- quake-specialised writer kernel: Cortex's own writer variants measured
  worse; the faces differ in shape (bigger polygons, deeper subdivision).
- baked materialise (inline asm over indexed corners): Cortex vertices are
  self-contained 12-byte PXBSP records; its Rust loop is ~17 instructions a
  vertex, an asm form would save two or three.
- scratchpad liquid phase, liquid half rate: Cortex has no liquids.
- sky sample cache: Cortex has its rotation-keyed sky packet cache.

So the transferable set is empty, and the per-primitive world cost is the
same order in both. Cortex's extra 80k instructions a frame is the skinned
model pipeline (joint transforms, blended vertices, per-vertex projection
and bounds), which quake does not have at all; that, not the world pass, is
where a Cortex-only lever would have to be.

The one thing quake still did the old way is the load-delay hazard: it
built with `-disable-mips-df-backward-search`, leaving a nop in 98% of its
branch delay slots, 352M of the route's 3,035M instructions (about 8% of
its work outside the vblank spin). Cortex and hl-psx let the filler run and
patch the hazards after the link. Ported to all three flag users tonight:

| game | main | work / cadence before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| quake-psx | trampoline commit on main | 1,859.2M work instructions, 24.916 fps | 1,788.2M (-3.8%), 25.136 fps, hashes identical, parity PASS, 9 sites patched |
| VoXide | main, repinned to `e4f27c2f` | spawn loop body 1,240,381 cycles | 1,203,806 (-2.9%), face loop 895k -> 864k |
| NitroXide | main, repinned to `e4f27c2f` | split route 99.6% of frames at two fields | unchanged (vblank-bound), 6 sites patched, headroom |

VoXide is now 61k cycles from the 30 fps quantum at spawn instead of 98k.

## Measured and dropped

- Write-time OT linking in the shared classic-affine writers (the packet
  is prepended to its slot as it is written, `ClassicAffineProfile` carrying
  the slot array; psx-bsp's renderer takes the pointer and editor-playtest
  drops the deferred `add_committed_tagged_packet_stream_unchecked` pass):
  +3.39% bus cycles, +1.97% work instructions, Cortex tape. The deferred
  pass left `Playtest::render` (114.6M -> 96.5M) but
  `submit_classic_affine_mixed_batch` went 204.6M -> 251.7M instructions
  and 39,192 -> 47,636 bytes: a load-modify-store inside every inlined
  emitter perturbs register allocation across the whole function (stack
  load stall share 9.35% -> 11.14%) and the 4 KB icache pays for the extra
  code. The hashes moved only through same-slot order between world and
  model packets (frames visually identical). A host test proved the two
  forms produce the same table. Reverted; tightening the asm pass instead
  (landed above) is the cheaper form of the same idea.

- Branch-free per-vertex plane scan in `draw_pxbsp_faces` (sign-spread
  masks instead of six instructions and a branch per plane): +0.08% bus
  cycles, -0.04% instructions, hash-identical. The scan's phase one rarely
  runs more than one vertex, so there was nothing to save. Reverted.

- `-Cllvm-args=-regalloc=pbqp` for the Cortex guest (hoping for fewer
  spills in the big render functions): +50% bus cycles, +293% instructions,
  hashes differ, with the hazard scanner reporting the image clean. The
  allocator breaks something LLVM's MIPS-I scheduling otherwise guarantees;
  not investigated further. The scanner now also counts straight-line
  load-use pairs as a warning so the next such experiment fails loudly.

## Measurement traps met tonight

- quake's chain bench reports wall cycles and a field-quantised fps: a
  work change that does not move a frame across a field boundary is
  invisible (24.842 fps and 2,291,807,0xx cycles for three different
  SDKs). Judge quake SDK changes by the `--pc-line-log` instruction profile
  (total minus `gpu_end_frame` and `SectorReader::read_sector`).
- `quake-psx-build --psoxide <dir>` overrides the SDK from that tree, and
  the guest stage under `/tmp/quake-psx-guest-v1/<recipe>` is keyed on the
  quake sources only: two worktrees with different `.psoxide` copies share
  one stage. Delete the stage before an SDK A/B.
- psoxide-link keeps source mtimes, so a hydrated SDK change does not
  rebuild the guest (VoXide measured three identical "before/after" pairs);
  touch the hydrated sources or clean the guest target.
- zsh `noclobber` is on in this shell: `>` to an existing log fails
  silently inside a background subshell. Use `>|`.
